## Recognising a scam in progress
A scam in progress is a payment made by the genuine customer, from their usual device and location, that is far larger than anything they have paid before and goes to a payee they have never paid. It is especially concerning if the customer is a senior citizen, the amounts escalate within minutes, or the payment note mentions a deposit, verification, clearance, a refund or a fine. The customer is often still on a call or video call with the fraudster while paying.

## Intervention script
1. Hold the payment (FraudShield's REVIEW decision) and call the customer on their registered number.
2. Ask open questions: who asked you to make this payment, and why?
3. State clearly that no police, CBI, customs, court or RBI official will ever demand money over UPI, and that "digital arrest" does not exist in Indian law.
4. If the customer confirms coercion or deception, decline the payment, help them report on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, and flag the payee account as a suspected mule.
5. If the customer insists the payment is genuine, document the conversation. Consider a cooling-off delay for very large first-time payments, per bank policy.

## After a loss
If money has already left, report immediately through the national cybercrime reporting channel so the receiving bank can place a hold. Treat the payee account as a suspected mule and open a linked case.
