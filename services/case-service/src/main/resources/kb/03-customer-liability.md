---
title: Customer Liability in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions
source: Summary of RBI circular "Customer Protection - Limiting Liability of Customers in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions" (July 2017). Verify against the current RBI text.
---

## Zero liability
A customer has zero liability for an unauthorised electronic transaction in two situations:
- The fraud is due to contributory fraud, negligence or deficiency on the part of the bank, whether or not the customer reports it.
- The breach is a third-party breach, where the deficiency lies neither with the bank nor with the customer but elsewhere in the system, and the customer notifies the bank within three working days of receiving the bank's communication about the transaction.

## Limited liability
For a third-party breach reported within four to seven working days, the customer's liability is limited to the transaction value or a cap, whichever is lower. The cap depends on the type of account (for example ₹10,000 for basic savings accounts and higher caps such as ₹25,000 for some other account types). If the customer reports after seven working days, liability is decided by the bank's Board-approved policy.

## Customer negligence
If the loss is due to the customer's own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, an OTP or a UPI PIN, the customer bears the entire loss until they report the unauthorised transaction to the bank. Any loss after the report is borne by the bank.

## Shadow reversal
When a customer reports an unauthorised transaction, the bank must credit the amount involved (shadow reversal) to the customer's account within 10 working days of the notification, without waiting for any insurance claim to be settled.

## What this means for investigators
- Record exactly when the customer was alerted and when they reported the transaction; liability depends on these dates.
- Distinguish unauthorised transactions (account takeover, where the customer did not make the payment) from authorised push payment scams (the customer made the payment under deception). The liability framework above is written for unauthorised transactions.
- Account takeover cases usually fall under the unauthorised-transaction framework. Digital arrest and other social-engineering scams usually do not, but the bank should still help the customer report the fraud and try to recover funds.
