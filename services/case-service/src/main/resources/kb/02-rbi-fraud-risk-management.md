## Scope and intent
In July 2024 the RBI issued revised Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management. There are separate texts for commercial banks (including RRBs) and All India Financial Institutions, for cooperative banks, and for NBFCs. They superseded the earlier master circulars on fraud classification and reporting. The directions put responsibility on the Board, require a Board-approved fraud risk management policy, and emphasise early detection, timely reporting and the principles of natural justice.

## Early Warning Signals and Red-Flagged Accounts
Regulated entities must have an Early Warning Signals (EWS) framework, integrated with their transaction monitoring and core banking systems, that surfaces potential fraud early. Accounts showing early warning signals can be classified as Red-Flagged Accounts (RFA) while the entity investigates. Data analytics and market intelligence should feed the EWS framework.

## Natural justice before declaring fraud
Before an account holder or entity is classified as a fraud, the regulated entity must issue a detailed show cause notice. The notice must set out the transactions, actions and events that form the basis for considering a fraud declaration. The person must be given at least 21 days to respond. The entity must have a formal system for issuing such notices and examining the responses. A case-management system should therefore record the evidence trail for every decision.

## Reporting obligations
Frauds must be reported promptly to the RBI through the prescribed fraud monitoring returns, and to law enforcement agencies where required. The exact timelines and formats depend on the type of entity and the amount involved. Investigators should follow the bank's compliance calendar and the current RBI text. The case record should capture who classified the fraud, when, and on what evidence, because these details are needed for the regulatory return.

## Governance
The directions require a committee of the Board to oversee fraud risk management, and periodic reviews of the fraud risk policy, the EWS framework and staff accountability. A dedicated fraud risk management function within senior management is expected to run day-to-day monitoring.
