## Victim reporting: 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in
Victims of cyber financial fraud in India should call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or file a complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) as soon as possible. Complaints flow into the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System. Through this system, banks and payment intermediaries are alerted so that fraudulent funds can be put on hold before they are layered further. Speed matters: money moved through mule accounts is usually withdrawn or dispersed within hours.

## Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR) to FIU-IND
Under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) rules, a reporting entity must file a Suspicious Transaction Report with the Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND). FIU-IND guidance asks for the STR within seven working days of the Principal Officer being satisfied that a transaction is suspicious. The Principal Officer should record the reasons for treating the transaction as suspicious. There should be no undue delay between an alert being raised and the suspicion being established. Customers must not be told that an STR has been filed (no tipping-off).

## When to file an STR from a FraudShield case
File, or escalate to the Principal Officer for filing, when:
- An account is confirmed as a mule account (fan-in from victims followed by rapid onward transfer).
- An account receives proceeds from a confirmed account takeover or scam.
- A pattern of probing or structuring is observed that has no plausible legitimate explanation.

## Internal escalation matrix
- P1 (blocked payment of ₹50,000 or more, mule alert with score of 0.85 or more, or a suspected scam in progress against a senior citizen): page the on-call investigator. The target response time is 15 minutes.
- P2 (any other block, or review of ₹10,000 or more): the target response time is 1 hour.
- P3 (other reviews): the target response time is 4 hours.
